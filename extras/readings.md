## Historical & Foundational Primary Texts

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [*Theaetetus*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1726) | Plato | Classic Dialogue | Originates Western analytical epistemology by systematically examining whether knowledge can be defined as perception, true judgment, or true judgment with an account (logos). |
| [*Meditations on First Philosophy*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/59) | René Descartes | Primary Text | Establishes modern foundationalism through radical methodic doubt, the Cogito argument, and the quest for indubitable epistemic bedrock. |
| [*An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/9662) | David Hume | Primary Text | Formulates the classical problem of induction, challenging rational justification for empirical generalisations and causal inferences. |
| [*Critique of Pure Reason*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/4280) | Immanuel Kant | Classic Treatise | Revolutionizes epistemology by synthesizing rationalism and empiricism, arguing that human cognition actively structures sensory experience via synthetic a priori categories. |
| [*“Proof of an External World” (1939)*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/4544854) | G. E. Moore | Landmark Essay | Proposes the canonical common-sense refutation of radical skepticism, illustrating the power of dogmatist counter-arguments against skeptical hypotheses. |

## The Analysis of Knowledge & the Gettier Revolution

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [*“Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?” (1963)*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3326922) | Edmund L. Gettier | Landmark Paper | A three-page milestone paper that famously dismantled the traditional tripartite definition of knowledge with counterexamples involving epistemic luck. |
| [*“A Causal Theory of Knowing” (1967)*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2023895) | Alvin I. Goldman | Landmark Paper | Introduces externalism to modern epistemology by arguing that knowledge requires an appropriate causal connection between fact and belief. |
| *[Philosophical Explanations](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780674664791)* (Chapter: “Knowledge and Skepticism”) | Robert Nozick | Monograph Chapter | Develops the influential counterfactual tracking account of knowledge (sensitivity and adherence conditions) to defeat Gettier cases and skeptical challenges. |
| [*“Solving the Skeptical Problem” (1995)*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2186011) | Keith DeRose | Landmark Paper | Provides the definitive contextualist framework, demonstrating how truth-conditions for knowledge attributions shift according to conversational contexts and epistemic standards. |

## Theories of Justification, Architecture, & Naturalism

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [*“Epistemology Naturalized” (1969)*](https://philpapers.org/rec/QUIEN) | W. V. O. Quine | Seminal Essay | Argues that traditional Cartesian normative epistemology should be abandoned and integrated into empirical psychological science as the study of physical cognitive inputs and theoretical outputs. |
| *[The Structure of Empirical Knowledge](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780674843813)* | Laurence BonJour | Monograph | The definitive contemporary defense of coherentist justification, presenting a rigorous alternative to foundationalist regress solutions. |
| *[Epistemology and Cognition](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780674258969)* | Alvin I. Goldman | Monograph | Synthesizes reliabilist epistemology with cognitive psychology, providing a comprehensive framework for process reliabilism. |
| *[Knowledge in Perspective: Selected Essays in Epistemology](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780521396431)* | Ernest Sosa | Collected Essays | Inaugurates virtue epistemology by introducing epistemic competence (virtues) as a way to transcend the stalemate between foundationalism and coherentism. |

## Social, Feminist, & Applied Epistemology

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *[Epistemic Injustice: Power and the Ethics of Knowing](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780198237907)* | Miranda Fricker | Monograph | A landmark work identifying testimonial injustice (credibility deficits rooted in prejudice) and hermeneutical injustice (structural gaps in collective interpretive resources). |
| *[Knowledge in a Social World](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780198238201)* | Alvin I. Goldman | Monograph | Systematizes social epistemology by evaluating how legal systems, scientific communities, and democratic voting structures maximize true belief over error. |
| *[Learning from Words: Testimony as a Source of Knowledge](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780199219162)* | Jennifer Lackey | Monograph | Provides a comprehensive account of testimony that balances internalist hearer requirements with externalist speaker reliability. |
| [*“White Ignorance” (2007)*](https://philpapers.org/rec/MILWI) | Charles W. Mills | Landmark Essay | Foundational text in non-ideal social epistemology examining how racial supremacy and structural oppression actively produce and sustain collective ignorance. |

## Knowledge-First & Contemporary Metaepistemology

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *[Knowledge and Its Limits](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780199256563)* | Timothy Williamson | Monograph | A transformative treatise that upends traditional analysis by establishing knowledge as a prime, unanalyzable mental state that sets the standard for evidence and assertion. |
| *[Knowledge and Practical Interests](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780199230433)* | Jason Stanley | Monograph | Defends pragmatic encroachment, demonstrating how practical stakes and decision-making contexts directly determine epistemic justification. |
| *[Putting Logic in Its Place: Formal Constraints on Rational Belief](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780199263257)* | David Christensen | Monograph | Investigates formal epistemology, examining the tension between deductive consistency and probabilistic degrees of rational belief (credences). |
| *[The Epistemology of Disagreement: New Essays](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780199698370)* | David Christensen & Jennifer Lackey (eds.) | Collected Essays | The definitive collection exploring the normative demands of peer disagreement, conciliationism, and steadfastness in epistemically divided societies. |
